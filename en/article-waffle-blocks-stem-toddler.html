STEM & play

First STEM discoveries with waffle blocks: connect, test and rethink

SUPUZZ Editorial Team · Published April 17, 2026

Connect a piece, take it off and try again. Children can notice shapes and balance through hands-on play before they know the word STEM. Start with curiosity rather than a lesson plan.

Begin with a few pieces

Make a small flat shape or enclosure. Give children time to find a connection and turn a piece around. A manageable starting point matters more than a complicated finished model.

Try one small experiment

Add a question to what children are already building.

  • Color: sort matching pieces or alternate two colors.
  • Balance: widen a base when a tall build falls over.
  • Space: use words such as above, below, beside and between.
  • Change: turn the same pieces into a wall, vehicle or creature.

Ask before offering the answer

What might make it steadier? Is there room for the car? Think through a next step together. When something does not work, try a different connection.

Follow an example or invent your own

Both guided builds and free construction have a place. Add names, destinations and characters to turn a finished model into pretend play.

Choose the actual product, not just the category

Check the selected set’s age guidance, piece sizes, warnings and contents. Cases and guides vary. Enjoy the activity at the child’s pace; it does not promise a particular developmental outcome.

Frequently asked questions

Do adults need STEM expertise?

No. Ask what might happen, try it together and put the child’s observations into words.

Can children under three use these blocks?

Check the age guidance and instructions of the specific product. Not every set is suitable for children under three.

How long should a session last?

There is no target duration. Follow the child’s interest and take a break or change activities when needed.
