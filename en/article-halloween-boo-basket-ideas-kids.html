Boo Basket Ideas for Kids: Build a Non-Candy Gift

By The SUPUZZ Team · September 22, 2026 · 4 min read

A boo basket is a small Halloween surprise presented in a basket, tote or box. For a non-candy version, start with one toy the child will use, add a note and plan a first activity. A container full of tiny extras is optional; one thoughtful gift can carry the whole idea.

A simple three-part boo basket

  1. One main gift. Choose a complete toy with an age label that fits the recipient.
  2. One personal detail. Add a drawing, a library book to read together or a note using the child’s name.
  3. One invitation. Write down a short activity you can do together when the basket is opened.

Choose the gift before the container. If the box will not fit inside a basket, put the note in a reusable tote and place the wrapped toy beside it. Keep the packaging and instructions together so the adult can check the contents. There is no need to repackage loose building pieces as mystery fillers.

Four themes with a first-play prompt

The friendly monster workshop

Use 80-piece Rainbow Waffle Blocks or the 128-piece version. Add a card reading, “Your monster needs a home. What will its front door look like?” Keep the story silly and let the child decide whether the monster has a name, a job or a pet.

The dinosaur expedition

Pair Dinosaur Jungle · L1 with a hand-drawn map showing a starting point and a party clearing. Invite the child to build a route between them. For more pieces, compare the L2 and L3 versions rather than buying an extra set just to fill the basket.

The underwater costume party

Choose Coral Reef L1 and draw a party invitation for a sea creature. The child can arrange a reef and invent who arrives first. The L2 and L3 versions offer other set sizes if a larger gift was already part of your plan.

The polar mystery

Choose Icy World L1, L2 or L3 after comparing age guidance and contents. Write, “Someone left an invitation in the snow. Where does it lead?” Avoid promising particular animals or accessories without checking the version you select.

Set a basket budget without buying filler

Decide on your total before shopping. Include the main gift, any wrapping and delivery costs. Reuse a container you already own, and make the note yourself. Prices change, so compare the current total for the selected set instead of assuming that a particular level is always cheaper.

For siblings, match the thought and attention rather than trying to make the boxes look identical. One child might want a dinosaur scene while another wants open-ended blocks. If they prefer a shared set, put both names on the card and give each person a role in the first build.

A ready-to-copy boo basket note

“You’ve been booed! Inside is a little invitation to make something wonderfully strange. Build a place for a friendly creature, give it a name, and tell me who comes to visit. Let’s make it together this weekend.”

For a neighbor’s child, ask the parent before leaving a surprise. Keep the note clear about who the gift is from and do not make passing the gift tradition along an obligation. For school gifts, check the teacher’s rules first.

Before ordering in the U.S. or Canada

Follow the selected package’s age guidance and check its contents before gifting. Product pages link to Amazon.com. Canadian shoppers should confirm delivery eligibility, currency, shipping charges and any import costs for their own address; a U.S. listing does not guarantee Canadian delivery. Check the current delivery estimate against your event date, not just October 31. Prices, stock and arrival dates can change.

Common questions

When should I give a boo basket?

Give it whenever it fits your family’s October plans. An early-month gift leaves time to use the activity before Halloween; giving it on Halloween works too.

Can I make a boo basket without buying lots of small toys?

Yes. One complete toy, a handwritten note and time to play together make a complete gift. Reuse a box or tote rather than buying a special container.
