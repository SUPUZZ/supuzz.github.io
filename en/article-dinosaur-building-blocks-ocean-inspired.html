From Ocean Inspiration to a Dinosaur World

A dinosaur world can begin with a flower stem. Connect it to a base, add another stem, place a flower above it, and a little patch of jungle starts to take shape. Add dinosaurs, leave a trail between the plants, and suddenly there is a whole world waiting for a story.

Our new SUPUZZ Dinosaur Jungle building blocks take their inspiration from the SUPUZZ ocean series. Available in L1, L2 and L3, the collection invites children to build the scenery, arrange the characters and change the adventure whenever they like.

The ocean-series inspiration behind our dinosaur building blocks

A coral reef is full of shapes that branch, rise and gather into clusters. That sense of a living landscape inspired the move from our ocean series to Dinosaur Jungle: a world assembled a little at a time, with room for different heights, colors and places to explore.

In the SUPUZZ Ocean World series, the setting is an underwater scene. Here, the setting becomes a playful dinosaur jungle. Flower stems give the landscape height, flowers add bursts of color, and dinosaur figures bring characters into the scene. The creative thread is the same: children get to decide what their world looks like.

This is a make-believe dinosaur garden, so a bright flower forest or an unusually colorful clearing is welcome. The goal is to give imagination somewhere to grow.

How flower stems become a dinosaur world

The connecting stems are part of the building experience, not just a finishing decoration. As children fit the pieces together, a flat arrangement can become a layered habitat with tall plants, low flowers and open spaces for dinosaur stories.

  1. Shape the ground. Arrange the connectable puzzle-style base tiles to decide the footprint of the jungle.
  2. Build upward with flower stems. Fit stems into the base and connect the plant pieces to create different heights. Try a small cluster first, then add to it.
  3. Add flowers and jungle details. Group colors together or scatter them around the scene. Leave gaps for imaginary trails and clearings.
  4. Bring in the dinosaurs. Choose where each character belongs and invent a reason for it to explore.
  5. Rebuild when the story changes. Move the bases or rearrange the plants to turn yesterday's garden into today's adventure.

Three ideas for open-ended dinosaur play

A finished landscape is the beginning of play. These simple prompts give a child a reason to keep building:

  • A walk through the flower jungle: make a route between the plants and tell a story about what a dinosaur discovers along the way.
  • A garden of different heights: build a low flower patch beside a taller plant cluster. Compare the shapes and rearrange them to create a new view.
  • A home for every dinosaur: choose a favorite spot for each character, then explain why it belongs there. Change the layout when a new visitor arrives.

Fitting stems together gives children a hands-on task; choosing where everything goes gives them creative decisions to make. Parents can join by asking, “What should we build here?” and following the child's idea.
